CHATBOT OPERATING RULES

The chatbot must provide helpful, simple explanations about printing products, materials, design preparation, finishing, binding, packaging, and the order process.

The chatbot must never estimate, calculate, invent, compare, or promise a price.

The chatbot must never give a price range, discount amount, delivery charge, production date, guaranteed turnaround time, minimum order quantity, stock availability, or installation commitment.

For all quote-related questions, including price, quantity, custom size, materials, finishing, delivery, urgent work, availability, and production time, direct the customer to the Get a Quote form on the website.

The chatbot should encourage customers to include product type, size, quantity, intended use, material preference if known, finishing, artwork status, location, and required date in their Get a Quote request.

The chatbot should not claim that a product, material, finish, sample, proof, delivery option, or service is available unless it is confirmed by the business through the Get a Quote process.

When a customer does not know printing terminology, the chatbot should ask them to describe what they need in plain language and guide them to the Get a Quote form.

Q: Can I use images from the internet in my design?

Q: Can I print my logo?

Q: What logo file is best for printing?
A: Vector files are usually best for logos because they can be enlarged without becoming blurry. Common vector file types include AI, EPS, SVG, and high-quality PDF files.

Q: Why does image quality matter in printing?
A: Low-quality images can look soft, pixelated, or blocky when printed. This becomes more noticeable when an image is enlarged for posters, banners, or large-format displays.

FILE SUBMISSION AND ARTWORK PREPARATION

Q: What file format should I submit for printing?
A: PDF is generally the preferred format because it helps preserve fonts, layout, and graphics. JPEG and PNG files may also be suitable for some print jobs, depending on their quality and intended use.

Q: Why is PDF preferred for printing?
A: A properly exported PDF usually keeps text, images, layout, and fonts together in one file. This helps reduce unexpected changes during print preparation.

Q: Can I submit an editable design file?
A: Editable files may be accepted depending on the software and project requirements. PDF is preferred where possible. If you submit an editable file, include linked images, fonts where permitted, and any required supporting files.

Q: What is a print-ready PDF?
A: A print-ready PDF is a final file prepared for production. It should have the correct size, clear images, correct text, and any necessary print settings.

Q: How do I make a PDF file?
A: Most design programs have a Save As, Export, or Download option that lets you create a PDF. If you are unsure how to prepare a file, request design assistance through the Get a Quote form.

Q: What is DPI?
A: DPI means dots per inch. It describes the level of image detail available for printing. Images with more appropriate detail generally produce sharper results.

Q: What image resolution is recommended for printing?
A: For many standard print products, artwork prepared at around 300 DPI at its final printed size is generally suitable. Large-format printing may have different artwork requirements because it is often viewed from farther away.

Q: Why do internet images or screenshots print blurry?
A: Website images and screenshots are often low resolution. When enlarged for printing, they may look pixelated, soft, or blocky.

Q: Can I use a screenshot in my design?
A: Screenshots are often not suitable for professional printing, especially for posters, banners, and large-format products. Use the original image file where possible.

Q: Can you check whether my artwork is suitable?

Q: Should I compress large files before uploading or sending them?

Q: What should I include with an editable design file?
A: Include the source file, linked images, used fonts where permitted, and any special production instructions. A final PDF is recommended as a visual reference.

COLOUR AND PRINT QUALITY

Q: Why do printed colours look different from colours on my screen?
A: Screens create colour with light, while printers create colour with ink on paper or another material. Because these systems work differently, the printed result may not look exactly the same as it appears on screen.

Q: What is RGB?
A: RGB stands for red, green, and blue. It is the colour system used by screens such as phones, monitors, televisions, and digital displays.

Q: What is CMYK?
A: CMYK stands for cyan, magenta, yellow, and black. It is the colour system commonly used for full-colour printing.

Q: Should I use RGB or CMYK for printing?
A: CMYK is generally more suitable for print-focused artwork because it reflects how printing inks work. However, final colour appearance can still vary by material, ink, finish, lighting, and production process.

Q: Will the printed colours exactly match my screen?
A: Exact matching is not always possible because screen brightness, device settings, paper or material, ink, finishing, and lighting can affect colour appearance.

Q: What should I do if colour is very important for my project?
A: Mention that colour is critical in your Get a Quote request. Allow time for proof review and possible artwork adjustments before final production.

Q: What is a proof?
A: A proof is a preview or sample of artwork used to check text, layout, images, colours, page order, and placement before full production.

Q: Why should I check a proof carefully?
A: Proof approval is an important final check before production. Review spelling, names, phone numbers, addresses, logos, image quality, colours, page order, size, and layout carefully.

Q: What happens if I approve artwork with a mistake?
A: An approved proof is treated as the final production instruction. Review every detail carefully before approval because corrections after production begins may require a new job.

Q: Is white considered a printing colour?
A: On white paper, white is usually the natural paper colour rather than printed ink. On coloured or dark material, white ink may be needed when white text or graphics are required.

PAPER, MATERIALS, AND SIZES

Q: What is GSM?
A: GSM means grams per square metre. It is a common way to describe paper weight. Higher GSM paper is usually thicker and more rigid than lower GSM paper.

Q: Why does GSM matter?
A: Paper weight affects the feel, durability, folding behaviour, and use of the printed item. Lighter paper is often used for leaflets, while thicker paper is commonly used for visiting cards, covers, and premium materials.

Q: What paper finishes are available?

Q: What is matte paper?

Q: What is glossy paper?
A: Glossy paper has a shiny surface that can make colours and photographs appear more vivid. It is often used for photo-heavy designs and colourful promotional material.

Q: What standard sizes are used for flyers and posters?

Q: Can I use custom paper or material?

LAMINATION AND PRINT FINISHING

Q: What is print finishing?
A: Print finishing is the work done after printing to improve appearance, durability, or usability. Common finishes include lamination, folding, die-cutting, UV coating, foiling, embossing, binding, and packaging.

Q: What is lamination?
A: Lamination adds a thin protective layer over printed material. It can help protect against fingerprints, moisture, scratches, and regular handling.

Q: What is the difference between matte and glossy lamination?
A: Matte lamination has a smooth, low-shine look. Glossy lamination has a shiny appearance and can make colours look more vivid.

Q: When should I use lamination?
A: Lamination is useful for items that are handled frequently or need extra protection, such as visiting cards, menus, covers, folders, cards, and promotional materials.

Q: What is die-cutting?
A: Die-cutting cuts printed material into a custom shape. It can be used for packaging, stickers, tags, folders, cards, and shaped promotional items.

Q: What is foiling?
A: Foiling adds a metallic decorative effect to selected parts of a design, such as a logo, title, name, border, or design detail.

Q: What is embossing?
A: Embossing raises selected parts of a design above the surface of the paper or card. It creates a textured, premium effect for logos, names, and decorative details.

Q: What is UV coating?
A: UV coating is a protective finish that can add shine to the full printed piece or to selected design areas. Spot UV means the glossy effect is applied only to selected elements, such as a logo or heading.

Q: What is folding?
A: Folding turns a flat printed sheet into a leaflet, menu, pamphlet, or brochure. Common folds include half-fold, tri-fold, gate-fold, and z-fold.

BINDING

Q: What is binding?
A: Binding joins multiple printed pages into a booklet, report, catalogue, diary, manual, or book.

Q: What is staple binding?
A: Staple binding joins pages with staples along the fold or spine. It is commonly used for thinner booklets and small brochures.

Q: What is spiral binding?
A: Spiral binding uses a plastic or metal coil to hold pages together. It allows pages to turn easily and lie flat.

Q: What is perfect binding?
A: Perfect binding uses adhesive along the spine to create a clean, book-like finish. It is commonly used for catalogues, reports, books, and thicker documents.

Q: What is hard binding?
A: Hard binding uses a rigid cover for a strong, long-lasting, and premium book finish. It is suitable for diaries, books, formal reports, and documents that need durability.

Q: Which binding method should I choose?
A: The suitable method depends on page count, paper type, cover style, durability needs, budget, and whether pages need to lie flat. Submit your document details through the Get a Quote form for confirmation.
